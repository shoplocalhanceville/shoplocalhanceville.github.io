Hanceville council adds $25,000 for road patching and upholds fire chief removal

Published Saturday, October 17, 2026 by Hanceville Today

At its Oct. 8 meeting the City Council put more gas tax money toward pothole repairs, debated who gets to take city trucks home and backed the mayor's removal of the fire chief. The next meeting is Thursday, Oct. 22, at 6 p.m.

If you have dodged a pothole on a Hanceville street lately, help is on the way. The City Council approved a $25,000 budget amendment on Oct. 8 so the Public Works Department can keep patching roads around town. The money comes from gas tax revenue, The Cullman Tribune reported.

Rusty Fields of Public Works told the council the patching has taken more time and material than expected because damaged spots vary in size and depth. City crews have been working with county crews and equipment at roughly $5,000 to $5,500 a day, and Fields said more than half of the planned work is done.

Take home vehicles drew the longest discussion. A proposed policy, Resolution 2026-8, would limit routine take home city vehicles to department heads and designated on call employees. Supporters pointed to fuel, insurance and upkeep costs, with one estimate putting six take home vehicles at about $17,000 a year. Public Works employees said keeping trucks at home helps them respond faster to fallen trees, flooding and storms. The council added emergency exceptions and a six month transition, then postponed a final vote until its next meeting.

Council members also talked about giving the council a bigger say in choosing the fire chief, public works director and park director. Mayor Nolan Bradford spoke against the change, and action was postponed for more review.

After an executive session of about 40 minutes, the council voted unanimously to uphold the mayor's removal of Fire Chief Chris Hardin, who had served since March. No public reason was given at the meeting and no replacement was announced, according to the Tribune. The Cullman Times also reported the vote.

In other business, the fire department answered 116 calls in September, the council gave $500 to the North Alabama Agriplex, Councilman Cameron Rowe was named to the Planning Commission, and work continues on the policies needed to bring back the Hanceville Police Department.

Want to be heard? The next council meeting is Thursday, Oct. 22, at 6 p.m.
