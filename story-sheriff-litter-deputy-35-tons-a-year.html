Public Safety

Sheriff's environmental deputy pulls up to 35 tons of trash a year off Cullman County roads

Published Friday, October 23, 2026 by Hanceville Today

Deputy Ron Wood and the sheriff's litter crew clean about 100 miles of county roads each year, and money from the recyclables they collect goes to local nonprofits.

If a county road near you looks cleaner than it used to, you may have Environmental Deputy Ron Wood to thank.

The Cullman County Sheriff's Office says Wood collects an estimated 50,000 to 70,000 pounds of roadside litter each year. That works out to about 25 to 35 tons from roughly 100 miles of roadway.

The program also gives back. Recyclable materials the crew picks up are sold, and the money is donated to nonprofits across Cullman County. In 2018, aluminum cans the crew collected brought in more than $1,300 for Cullman Caring for Kids.

The sheriff's office says the work has earned repeated recognition from Alabama People Against a Littered State, known as Alabama PALS, through its Governor's Awards law enforcement category.

Sheriff Matt Gentry praised Wood's dedication, saying the work keeps roads clean, protects the environment and helps local nonprofits. You can help too: secure truck loads and keep trash in the vehicle until you reach a can.
