Cullman County remembers Eddie Wilson, who built The Foundry Farm into a place for men to rebuild their lives

Published Friday, October 23, 2026 by Hanceville Today

The longtime director of The Foundry Ministries' farm between Holly Pond and Berlin died Oct. 28. Visitation is today from noon to 2 PM at The Foundry Farm Chapel, with a celebration of life at 2 PM.

Eddie Wilson, the program director who spent more than a decade growing The Foundry Ministries' Cullman County farm, died unexpectedly on Wednesday, Oct. 28, The Cullman Tribune reported. He was 62 and lived in Hartselle.

Wilson started serving with The Foundry in 2006 and took over as director of its farm campus between Holly Pond and Berlin in 2014. The farm is a recovery program for men, offering counseling, education, job preparation, spiritual guidance and aftercare.

Under his leadership the farm added gardens, high tunnels, a hen house stocked with 300 laying hens, an 80 bed dormitory called Black-Sanders Hall and a chapel built with donated money, materials and labor. The North Alabama Agriplex said Wilson took a small farm class at Wallace State so he could teach residents, and the farm now grows enough vegetables and eggs to share with other Foundry campuses.

Wilson often spoke about his own recovery through The Foundry and said the farm work taught men habits they could take home, like showing up on time and caring for their work. "We want to give them hope that they can take back to their families," he told Alabama Poultry magazine in 2021.

Visitation is today, Saturday, Oct. 31, from noon to 2 PM at The Foundry Farm Chapel, 575 County Road 1676, Cullman, with a celebration of life at 2 PM. The service will also be livestreamed on The Foundry Ministries' Facebook Page. In place of flowers, the family asks for donations to the Eddie Wilson Memorial Scholarship Fund through The Foundry Ministries, which will help men seeking recovery.
