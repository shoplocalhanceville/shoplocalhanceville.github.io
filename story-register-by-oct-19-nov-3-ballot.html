Register to vote by Monday, Oct. 19, and get to know the five amendments on the Nov. 3 ballot

Published Saturday, October 17, 2026 by Hanceville Today

Monday is the last day to register for the Nov. 3 general election. Cullman County voters will also decide four statewide amendments and one local question about freezing property values for qualifying seniors.

If you want a say in the Nov. 3 general election, Monday, Oct. 19, is the day to circle. Alabama Secretary of State Wes Allen says that is the last day to register to vote. You can register online through the Secretary of State's website until 11:59 p.m. that night, mail a form postmarked by Monday, or turn one in to the Cullman County Board of Registrars before the office closes.

Other dates are close behind. The state voter guide lists Oct. 27 as the last day to apply for an absentee ballot by mail and Oct. 29 as the last day to apply in person. Absentee ballots returned by mail must arrive by noon on Election Day.

Below the races for office, every voter in Cullman County will see five yes or no questions. Statewide Amendment 1 combines three changes in one vote. It would let voters fill a lieutenant governor vacancy that happens early in a term, let the Legislature set pay for the lieutenant governor and legislative officers by joint resolution, and require a legislative officer to approve travel spending.

Statewide Amendment 2 would set up a process for neighboring county school systems to merge under one board, with a state impact study and a way for voters to protest. The Cullman Tribune notes it would not automatically combine Cullman City Schools and Cullman County Schools.

Statewide Amendment 3 would require local school boards to adopt policies for the Pledge of Allegiance at the start of each school day and to allow voluntary prayer that students start and lead. Statewide Amendment 4 would require public schools to play or perform the first verse of The Star Spangled Banner at least once a week.

The local question would let homeowners age 65 and older freeze the assessed value of their home for property taxes. The home must have been their main residence for at least five years, and there is no income limit. Claims could start Oct. 1, 2027, and a tax bill could still change if millage rates change.

You can read the official Fair Ballot Commission statement for each statewide amendment on the Secretary of State's website, and the Cullman County Probate Office posts a sample ballot before Election Day. Questions about the senior property freeze can go to the Revenue Commissioner's Office at 256-775-4776.
